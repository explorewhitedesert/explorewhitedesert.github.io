How to read the calendar

  • For the starry sky: pick any night within about four days of a new moon. The galactic core is highest in the evening sky from March to October; in winter you get Orion, the Geminid and Quadrantid meteor showers and the longest, darkest nights instead.
  • For the glowing white landscape: pick the nights right around a full moon. The formations cast long moon shadows and you can walk around camp without a flashlight.
  • For meteor showers: 2027 gives the Eta Aquariids (May 5-6, on a new moon) the best conditions. The famous Perseids (Aug 12-13) fight a bright moon and the heat; the Geminids (Dec 13-14) collide with the full moon this year, so only the brightest fireballs will show.
  • For comfortable camping: October to April is the season, with December and January the coldest and most popular nights.

What a stargazing night with us looks like

You leave Cairo in the morning, reach Bahariya Oasis for lunch, then cross the Black Desert, Crystal Mountain and Agabat Valley by 4x4 into the White Desert. Camp is set before sunset. After dinner cooked on the fire, the sky takes over: no towns, no roads, no lights, just the stars over the chalk formations. Sunrise and breakfast, then back to Cairo by late afternoon.

Two nights free in a row around a new moon? Ask about staying a second night: the sky is different every night.
