The wildlife of the White Desert

Explore White Desert · November 2026

The White Desert looks empty, but it is full of life that hides from the midday sun. Most of it you will never see - but the tracks around camp at dawn tell the night's story.

The foxes

Two fox species live here. The fennec - the tiny desert fox with enormous ears - is the famous one, and the hardest to see: strictly nocturnal and shy. More common is Rüppell's sand fox, which sometimes trots through camp after dark, drawn by the smell of dinner. Sit quietly by the fire and your chances are good.

Gazelles and other mammals

The Dorcas gazelle lives in the open plains and wadis. You will not see herds, but a pair crossing in the distance at dawn is a real possibility, and their tracks are everywhere. There are also desert hares, jerboas (the hopping desert mouse), and the occasional sand cat - though seeing one takes serious luck.

Birds

The desert is liveliest in spring and autumn, when migrants pass between Europe and Africa: wheatears, warblers, shrikes and sometimes hoopoe larks around the oasis gardens. Year-round residents include the white-crowned wheatear, desert larks and ravens. Over Bahariya, look up for migrating storks and raptors in season.

Reptiles and the small world

Lizards are the animals you will actually see: agamas doing push-ups on warm rocks and sand-colored geckos after dark. The horned viper lives here too - it is venomous, beautifully camouflaged, and wants nothing to do with you. Our guides check camp areas, and the rule is simple: never put your hands or feet where you cannot see, and shake out shoes in the morning.

How to watch

Walk quietly at dawn, look for tracks in the soft sand around camp, and scan the middle distance rather than the ground at your feet. Bring binoculars if you have them. And remember: everything here is protected - we look, photograph, and leave the desert exactly as we found it.
